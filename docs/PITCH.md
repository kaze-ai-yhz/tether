# Tether: Pitch Deck Wording

Oct 23, 2026. Two slides of wording. Every number is sourced or labeled as an illustration.

## Slide 1: What falling through the cracks costs

**Chronic disease is managed at home, and almost no one is checking in.**

- **Clinicians are out of hours.** A primary care doctor would need 26.7 hours a day to deliver all recommended care, 7.2 of them for chronic disease alone. Follow-up calls are the first thing to go.
- **Preventable admissions are expensive.** 3.5 million adult hospital stays a year are potentially preventable, costing $33.7 billion, or 8.9% of all hospital costs. Diabetes accounts for $7.4 billion of that and COPD for $7.3 billion.
- **Lives are shorter.** Patients with diabetes who do not take their medications as prescribed have 81% higher odds of dying.
- **Work and independence are lost.** COPD alone costs 16.4 million lost workdays a year. Diabetes costs $106.3 billion a year beyond medical bills, in lost productivity, missed work, disability and early death.
- **Families carry the rest:** emergency trips, time off to care for a parent, and lost wages.

In low-income communities the gap is widest and the staff is thinnest.

## Slide 2: Tether keeps patients connected between visits

**Tether does the follow-up a practice cannot staff, and hands the clinician only the decisions.**

It watches home readings and refills, applies AHA/ACC, GOLD and ADA guidelines, and reaches patients by reminder and AI check-in call. A physician approves every action.

| Who | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Clinicians | Routine reminders, check-in calls and adherence checks run automatically. Clinicians review a short queue of drafted decisions, each one click to approve. |
| Patients | Problems caught at home, weeks earlier. Adherent diabetes patients had about a third fewer hospitalizations, and stays shorter by almost a day. |
| Life expectancy and quality | Taking medications as prescribed is linked to lower mortality. More stable days, fewer exacerbations, more independence. |
| Families | Fewer emergencies, fewer days off to drive a parent to the ER, less caregiver strain. |
| Work and disability | Fewer lost workdays and less disease-driven disability, keeping people employed. |
| Hospitals, states and society | Fewer avoidable admissions, many paid by Medicare and Medicaid. Every 2% reduction in preventable admissions saves about $674 million a year nationally. |

**Tether turns a missed follow-up into a phone call instead of a hospital bed.**

## Notes before presenting

- **The 2% figure is an illustration:** 2% of the $33.7 billion spent on preventable admissions (2017 data). Say "every 2% reduction," not "Tether saves."
- **Mortality is an association.** The diabetes study found higher odds of death with nonadherence; it does not prove that raising adherence adds a set number of years. Say "linked to."
- **Hospital savings, not total savings.** Adherent diabetes patients spent less on hospital and ER care but more on drugs, so total cost was about even in that study. Lead with fewer admissions and better lives.
- **Clinician time:** Tether's time saving is not yet measured. Keep it qualitative until a pilot measures it.
- **Data years:** COPD workdays are 2010 CDC data; preventable admissions are 2017; diabetes costs are 2022.
